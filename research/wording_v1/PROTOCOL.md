# Routing wording diagnostic, v1

This experiment was frozen after the original policy results and post-hoc routing inspection, before evaluating any model on these new states. It tests the specific hypothesis that explicit English wording of a two-field conjunction improves action selection when exactly one field is true. It does not retroactively confirm the original broad Chinese-vs-English effect.

The generator uses new seeds 404, 505, and 606. Each seed has eight unique states for each of the four `(outage, paying)` truth-table cells. All 96 base states are disjoint from the original policy diagnostic by the combined facts-and-parameters fingerprint. Categories and thresholds vary; labels come from the executable routing oracle and a separate declarative oracle. Four matched conditions keep facts, parameters, candidate labels/descriptions, and the English action question fixed. Within each language, only the first clause of `state.policy` changes: compact conjunction vs an explicit statement that both booleans must be true. This yields 384 requests and 384 action decisions per system.

Primary estimand: explicit-minus-compact action accuracy on the 48 XOR states in English, per model. Secondary estimands: English overall wording effect, Chinese wording effect on XOR states, and difference of the English and Chinese wording effects. Also report absolute accuracy in every truth-table cell and all failures. The three seeds replicate generated data, not model training. Paired 95% intervals use 10,000 cluster bootstrap draws stratified by seed and truth-table cell, seed 20260929. No prompt selection, trial rerun, or failure repair is allowed after scores are observed.

The within-English comparison isolates the wording clause in the supplied policy. The cross-language comparison also changes rendering and adapter language configuration; it cannot identify a general language advantage. The Chinese formulations are authored and require independent bilingual validation for a stronger semantic-equivalence claim. This is a synthetic rule-following diagnostic, not evidence about observed customer outcomes.
