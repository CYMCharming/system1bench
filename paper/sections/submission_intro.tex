\section{Introduction}
An application that routes a customer request or flags an evidence claim consumes a particular selected action. Two systems with the same accuracy may fail on different cases; a single system may preserve its accuracy while changing many actions when an equivalent candidate set is rendered differently. For a typed decision interface, reliability therefore has at least two coordinates: agreement with an appropriate reference and stability under a specified intervention. Neither can be inferred from the other.

This question is not new in isolation. Behavioral tests formalize invariance and directional expectations \citep{checklist}; broad evaluations emphasize multiple metrics \citep{helm}; option identifiers, order and answer-selection procedures have known effects \citep{mcq,benchmarksensitivity,firsttoken}. Recent legal and computational-social-science studies also compare decision models with language models across reference quality, corrections, regressions, calibration and cost \citep{legaldecisions,cssdecisions}. JevAdvBench already tests typed perturbations and repeated requests \citep{jevadvbench}. The contribution here is an auditable \emph{combination}: source-specific reference provenance, matched correctness transitions across native heads and fixed language-model adapters, orthogonal interface controls, and a separate external-domain check. We do not claim to be the first cross-domain or order-sensitivity benchmark.

The historical atlas contains 15 source collections and 28 main suites; its 8 additional controls reuse source cases. Because labels range from dataset annotations to authored policies and synthetic teachers, their percentages are never averaged into one ability score. We subsequently froze two new diagnostics before their respective inference: 288 executable-policy base states, and 264 pairs from legal contracts and scientific abstracts. A separately frozen three-class SciFact extension selected 180 cases; after inspecting it, a fresh, post-hoc census covered all 339 eligible cited pairs. None is silently counted as a historical suite. The model-facing object throughout is a supplied state and typed question map. Our unit is a \emph{model plus adapter on a fixed downstream decision}, not a deployed legal, scientific or customer-service system.

Three empirical observations structure the paper. First, exact repeats and candidate reversals expose prediction changes that net accuracy can hide. Second, independent legal and scientific sources show that correctness turnover varies across source--task--adapter configurations; the three-class science check further exposes cancellation across reference classes. Third, a factorial codebook control demonstrates that ordinary reversal in a single-token language-model adapter jointly changes semantic display and code assignment. The final section examines prompting, source validity and statistical boundaries rather than treating any favorable rendering as a universal repair.
