Typed decision interfaces turn a supplied state into finite choices that an application can act upon, yet a single accuracy score hides both source heterogeneity and sensitivity to equivalent presentations. System1Bench evaluates five fixed model--adapter systems across 15 source collections (28 main suites and eight matched controls), then tests independently sourced legal and scientific evidence pairs. We preserve domain, decision function and reference provenance, and report paired correctness, prediction transitions, invalid outputs and efficiency instead of pooling incompatible labels. On 144 balanced ContractNLI test pairs, candidate reversal switches 66 Llama and 64 Qwen labels, even as their reference accuracies rise by 10.4 and 12.5 percentage points. In a post-hoc census of all 339 eligible SciFact cited pairs, Qwen's total reference-correct count changes only $231\to220$, while no-information correct falls $112\to68/130$; a fresh 159-pair same-source complement to an earlier balanced selection preserves that class-level loss. A 2$\times$2 legal control shows that the same apparent reversal hides different adapter couplings: answer-code remapping alone switches 125/144 Llama labels but 10/144 Qwen labels; display reversal alone switches 6/144 and 36/144. Exact-score ties require an explicit common decoding rule for interpreting that control. A document-blind, supervised hypothesis prior reaches 56.9\% on the selected legal cases, underscoring source-shortcut risk. The artifact retains source-level denominators, raw predictions and executable references. These findings characterize typed downstream decisions, not end-to-end system competence.
