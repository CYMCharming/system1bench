Typed decision interfaces make finite choices, binary probabilities and ordinal judgments that applications can consume directly. We introduce System1Bench, a cross-domain evaluation of five model--adapter systems over 15 source collections, 28 main suites and eight matched controls. A source atlas distinguishes application domains, decision functions and reference provenance; heterogeneous labels are never pooled into one intelligence score. Paired measurements separate prediction discordance from correctness turnover: on Banking77, candidate reversal changes 38\% of Laya Multilingual decisions despite zero net accuracy change. In a frozen 288-state executable-policy experiment, orthogonal display and answer-code interventions expose cancellations invisible to ordinary reversal. A further held-out 96-state wording test finds that making a conjunction explicit changes Jev's English XOR-case accuracy from 7/48 to 13/48, but its Chinese accuracy from 46/48 to 13/48. The latter result rules out a language-independent wording explanation of the earlier routing pattern. A separate 98,304-decision local experiment measures resident-GPU performance; hosted latency remains a distinct boundary. Raw predictions, executable references, paired uncertainty estimates and figure code support an auditable evaluation of correctness, stability and efficiency at a fixed downstream decision interface, rather than of whole-system task completion.
